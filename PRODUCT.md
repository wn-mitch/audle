# Product

## Register

product

## Users

People age 9 and up, from curious first-time beat makers to experienced electronic-music creators, making a short daily session on a phone or desktop. They need an immediate, musical result without needing production knowledge, while retaining precise controls when they want them.

## Product Purpose

Audle makes, shares, hears, and lets people choose a favorite constrained daily loop. Everyone receives the same sixteen-source kit for their local day. Play opens on the sixteen sound objects: the first tap starts a loop, later taps bring parts in or out on the next bar, and the selected sound offers six pattern feels, a one-step offset, and optional tuning. Play also records a live take of the mute and solo switches made while the loop runs, kept separate from the editable loop. Arrange exposes the precision timeline, independent cloned voices, and Jev (TypeSafe's choice model), which fills empty tracks from a vetted pattern catalog steered by an optional vibe. Sharing is one sheet reachable from Play and Arrange: copy a loop or take link, download either as WAV, and reopen loops shared to this device. A share link is a short `/s/…` URL held by the share service for 90 days; if that service is unavailable, sharing falls back to the self-contained `#audle=…` link the loop always carries, which never expires. A shared loop can be remixed into the maker's own draft. Within five minutes, a new maker should be able to create a layered, recognizably authored result and send it.

## Brand Personality

Buoyant, tactile, clever. The instrument rewards curiosity with sound and visible change, treats people as capable, and remains credible for serious music-making.

## Anti-references

- Full desktop DAW complexity
- Neon cyberpunk club UI
- Patronizing children's software
- Generic card dashboard
- Retro handheld, toy-shadow, or cream-plastic interfaces

## Motion register

Motion conveys state and rewards a gesture: press compression, a flash on the pad whose sound just played, a beat pulse, a queued-change cue. One exception is deliberate: the sound field and pad bank enter with a short diagonal stagger once per page load, so the instrument arrives with a sense of physical presence. That entrance is the only orchestrated sequence in the product; nothing else animates on load, and all of it disappears under reduced motion.

## Design Principles

1. Sound on the first gesture.
2. Teach by remixing, never by blocking creation.
3. Every tap should reward with an audible or visible result.
4. Start shallow, reveal optional depth.
5. Constraints create comparability, while cloned voices preserve expression.
6. Touch and keyboard are peers.
7. A shareable personal result belongs within five minutes.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Meet WCAG 2.2 AA. Support reduced motion, non-color cues, complete keyboard operation, and no gesture-only command. Primary touch targets are at least 48 by 48 CSS pixels; compact controls are at least 44 by 44. Audio requires an explicit user gesture, and shared links fail safely with clear recovery language.
